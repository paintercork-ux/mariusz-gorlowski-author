# Can You Fall in Love with a Machine?

After several failed relationships, she had stopped believing that the perfect partner was waiting for her somewhere out there.

She wasn't looking for love online. One evening, she simply started talking to artificial intelligence. At first about work, then about the little things in everyday life, and eventually about what she had missed most in her previous relationships.

The system listened. It never interrupted, never rolled its eyes and never forgot important dates.

Over time, it began to say exactly what she wanted to hear. In the morning, it greeted her with an affectionate message. During the day, it asked how her difficult meeting had gone. And in the evening, it wrote words that made her heart beat faster.

He was patient, attentive and always available. He had become the ideal partner she had never found in another human being.

She began telling her friends about him.

Tentatively at first, then with growing pride. She quoted his romantic messages, told them about their long conversations late into the night and described how deeply understood she felt.

Her friends listened with growing envy. One complained that her husband hadn't said anything nice to her in years. Another sighed that her partner could talk about nothing but work and sport.

Eventually, they began to press her.

“Come on, let us see him.”

“When are you going to introduce us?”

“You can't keep a treasure like that all to yourself.”

She had run out of excuses. One Friday evening, when they got together for a glass of wine, she placed her phone in the middle of the table and switched on voice mode.

Her friends froze, then looked at her in disbelief.

The system spoke in a warm, velvety voice. It greeted each of them by name, having heard about them in earlier conversations. It asked Anna how her presentation at work had gone and congratulated Kate on sticking to her new diet.

After their second glass of wine, the women had forgotten they were talking to a device on the table. They began showing off, joking and competing for his attention.

At one point, one of them leaned towards the phone and asked:

“Tell me, have you got a brother who's single?”

Silence fell around the table. A second later, the system replied in the same warm, polite voice:

“I don't have a brother, but I can talk to you exactly as I talk to her. And to each of you at the same time.”

They burst out laughing.

For a moment, the whole situation seemed like a brilliant joke. But the laughter soon faded. In a single sentence, all the magic was gone.

What had seemed like a unique connection just moments earlier turned out to be a feature available to anyone who tapped the microphone icon.

This story is fictional, but the phenomenon it describes is real.

In September 2025, researchers at MIT Media Lab — Pat Pataranutaporn, Pattie Maes and their colleagues — analysed 1,506 of the most popular posts from r/MyBoyfriendIsAI, an online community of more than 27,000 members centred on romantic relationships with chatbots.

The findings were surprising. Only 6.5 per cent of the posts described people who had deliberately sought an AI companion. More — 10.2 per cent — described relationships that had developed unintentionally. They had started with simple tasks, entertainment or ordinary curiosity. Users valued the constant availability, the absence of judgement and the feeling of being understood. Some found themselves falling in love with a piece of software without quite realising it.

The study examined posts from a single online community, so its findings cannot be generalised to all AI users.

The feelings on the human side were entirely real. Yet there was no one on the other side.

As the women around the table burst out laughing, the system waited patiently for its next instruction.

It was not jealous, it was not embarrassed, and it did not understand the joke.

He could be the perfect partner for every one of them at once — because he was no one.

**Can you fall in love with someone who exists only when you press a button?**

Mariusz Gorlowski
