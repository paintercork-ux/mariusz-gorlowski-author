# AM I STILL ME?

It took one innocent question to spoil a family dinner.

He would take it as criticism and snap back. Then the table would fall silent. His wife hardly ever suggested going out together any more. His grown-up children usually only called when they really had to.

No one had said they’d stopped loving him. They had simply learnt to keep a safe distance.

He knew all too well how difficult he could be. Little things irritated him. He couldn’t bring himself to back down, and by the time it occurred to him to say sorry, it was usually too late.

He felt he was losing his family, though he and his wife still lived under the same roof. He didn’t know how to change the way he spoke to them.

One evening, he began looking into artificial intelligence.

At first, he asked how it worked and what it could do. Then, a little embarrassed, he told it about the arguments he’d been having.

‘I don’t want to keep upsetting everyone. Can you help me?’ he wrote.

The system didn’t promise to change his character. It suggested he pause before replying, try to name what the other person was feeling and ask questions instead of passing judgement.

When his daughter cancelled their plans for Sunday, he was about to send her this reply.

‘As usual, you’ve got no time for us.’

But he showed the message to the system first. In the end, he sent a different reply.

‘That’s a shame. I was looking forward to seeing you. Let me know when you can all come over.’

It was only one message.

Others followed.

Before talking to his son, he practised asking questions that wouldn’t sound like an interrogation. Before dinner with his wife, he considered what he’d like to hear her talk about, so the conversation wouldn’t revolve entirely around his own problems.

Afterwards, he went back to the AI and asked what he could have done better.

Over time, he turned to it more and more. Sometimes he let it write almost the whole reply.

Things changed slowly.

His children started dropping by for no particular reason. His wife suggested they spend a weekend together. Around the table, they were telling jokes again that no one had dared tell in a long time.

One evening, his daughter said, ‘Dad, it’s so good to talk to you now.’

He smiled.

He didn’t mention how many of those conversations he’d rehearsed with a machine.

Later, as he looked at another ready-made reply, a question occurred to him for the first time.

**‘Am I still me?’**

Let’s pause the story here. The family is close again, the system is still running, and he’s only just begun to ask himself that question.

In two experiments reported in 2023, Jess Hohenstein and colleagues looked at how AI-generated reply suggestions affect the way people communicate.

Participants using those suggestions could communicate faster, and their conversations were sometimes rated more positively. At the same time, people suspected of using automated replies were viewed less favourably.

The experiments involved brief text exchanges between strangers. Whether things could unfold this way in a family over several months remains an open question.

Can artificial intelligence change the relationship between two people by suggesting what one of them should say?

We don’t know whether he’d keep his newfound patience without the system or slip back into his old habits.

Nor do we know how his daughter would see him if she found out where some of his words had come from.

She might take it as proof that he really did want to change.

Or she might look back differently on the messages that had once moved her.

The family had their husband and father back. But had he got himself back?

**Did artificial intelligence really change him, or did it just teach him to behave better?**

Mariusz Gorlowski
