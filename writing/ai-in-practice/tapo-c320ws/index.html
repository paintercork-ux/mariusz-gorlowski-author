Two Years Under the Watchful Eye of AI

TP-Link Tapo C320WS — an independent two-year review

Mariusz Gorlowski27 November 2026Bought with my own money

At around €53, is this camera up to the job of everyday home monitoring? And can AI tell the difference between something happening and something worth alerting us to?

Not every encounter with artificial intelligence begins with a chatbot. Sometimes it starts with a small camera on the outside of a house. I've been using the TP-Link Tapo C320WS for around two years, having bought it with my own money. This is neither an unboxing nor a sponsored review. It's an account of what the camera is like to live with.

In this series, I want to look beyond the manufacturer's advertising and see how technology works in real life. What genuinely makes our lives easier? Where does the technology fall short? And where is human judgement still needed?

01Price and value for money

One of the strongest arguments in this camera's favour is its price. I've paid more for cameras from lesser-known brands. For around €53, the Tapo offers 2K QHD resolution, colour night vision, event detection and a choice of local or cloud storage.

I compared ten Irish retail listings and found an average advertised price of €53.33. Most fell between roughly €44 and €58; one listing at €89 pushed up the average. These figures are a snapshot of listings and cached retailer pages available on 27 November 2026, excluding delivery, rather than a market-wide average. Prices and availability may change.

02Build and weather resistance

After two years outdoors, my camera's housing is still in very good condition. It looks as though I installed it far more recently. TP-Link rates it IP66: dust-tight and protected against powerful water jets, but not designed for immersion.

The lens has held up particularly well. I haven't had the morning condensation problems I experienced with a Ring camera I used previously, when I had to wipe the lens to get a clear picture. That is a comparison of the cameras I've owned, not a controlled test of either manufacturer's entire range.

03Power and connectivity

The C320WS runs from the mains via a 9 V power adapter. That's a real advantage for me. I've tried battery-powered cameras and cameras with solar panels, but had to keep an eye on their charge. In my experience, they sometimes ran down too quickly. With the Tapo, there's no battery to keep recharging.

The trade-off is that you need to get power to the camera in the first place. There is no built-in battery backup, so it stops working if the power goes off. For the network connection, you have the choice of 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi or Ethernet, provided the necessary cables can be routed safely.

04Image quality — day and night

The picture is good rather than outstanding. At 2560 × 1440 pixels, it is detailed enough for my day-to-day needs. In favourable conditions, I can read a number plate or recognise a face in the footage. That is not guaranteed in every situation: distance, movement, weather, lighting and recording settings all make a difference.

The low-light Starlight sensor and colour night vision are useful features. Infrared night vision is also available, with a claimed range of up to around 30 metres. Some detail is lost in rain or difficult lighting. Digital zoom lets me take a closer look at part of the footage, but it is no substitute for optical zoom.

The camera is fixed in position. The V2 specification lists a field of view of 97° horizontally and 113° diagonally. Having used other cameras, I would prefer a wider view and the ability to move the camera remotely through the app.

05What does artificial intelligence actually do here?

Conventional motion detection reacts to changes in the picture. AI-assisted detection goes a step further by classifying what appears in it. The C320WS can distinguish people and vehicles from some other sources of motion. It does not recognise individuals by their faces, nor can it tell why someone is there.

TP-Link also lists motion detection, line-crossing alerts, intrusion detection within a defined area and camera tampering detection. These are monitoring and automation features; not all of them necessarily use machine learning. Unlike generative AI, this system does not create text or images. It analyses footage from its sensor and responds according to its settings.

06Setup and everyday use

The smart alerts have turned out to be both the most interesting part of the camera and the one that takes the most work to get right. I use the camera in a busy area. Even after trying to narrow the detection zone, I was receiving so many alerts that I ended up limiting some detection and notification functions. Getting the camera to alert me to the things that actually mattered took longer than I had expected.

I can still go back through the recorded events, including clips in which animals appear. That does not mean the camera has a dedicated animal detection model, or that it will capture every event. In a quieter setting, the smart alerts may be much more useful than they have been in mine.

The app also provides a live view, event history, two-way audio and a light and sound alarm. It is worth remembering that a camera may capture other people as well as our own property. I have limited the visibility of private details in my video.

07Subscription and video storage

At the moment, Tapo Care costs me €3.79 a month, or €45.48 a year if the rate stays the same. I use it to store event recordings in the cloud and look back at them in the app. That is my own subscription price, not necessarily what a new customer would pay.

TP-Link specifies 30 days of event recordings for its Premium plan and seven days for Basic where that plan is available. Cloud storage is for events, not round-the-clock recording. For continuous recording, you can use a suitable microSD card instead. The V2 family supports cards up to 512 GB, according to the manufacturer; the card is sold separately. Basic live viewing and detection do not require Tapo Care.

You do not need a cloud subscription to use the camera. Whether it is worth the extra monthly expense depends on your needs and where you prefer to keep your recordings.

08Strengths and limitations

Strengths

  • Very good value for money
  • Good image quality for everyday use
  • Colour night vision
  • Housing still in good condition; no lens fogging in my unit
  • Mains power
  • Intelligent detection and local storage without a subscription

Limitations

  • Image could be sharper
  • Limited field of view and no pan or tilt
  • Time required to fine-tune settings
  • Too many alerts in busy surroundings
  • Additional cost of optional cloud storage

09Is it still worth buying?

After two years of use, I still think the C320WS represents very good value for money. It is a sensible choice if you want an affordable outdoor camera, have access to mains power and do not need remote pan or tilt. If you need a much wider view, remote movement or the ability to reliably make out fine details from a distance, another model may be a better fit.

I've given it four stars rather than five because the picture could be sharper, the field of view is limited and the detection settings take time to get right. The rating reflects my experience with one camera; it is not the result of laboratory testing of its security performance.

10An author's reflection

In The Transition Code, I explore how readily we delegate tasks to machines. We do it not because we are forced to, but because it makes our lives easier. A security camera is a simple example: we can leave a device watching a space rather than watching it ourselves.

But detecting an event is not the same as judging its significance. A camera can spot movement and classify an object; it cannot know whether that particular event is something we should worry about. Technology can take a burden off our shoulders, yet it can also give us so many signals that we start to ignore them.
