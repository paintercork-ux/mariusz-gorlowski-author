North America

Who can pull the plug on your business?

You get funding to develop AI. The contract requires you to spend a large share of it on cloud services sold by the investor. The US Federal Trade Commission documented deals like these. If your business runs on that service, who decides what it will cost you tomorrow?

South America

An endorsement that never happened

Famous faces appear to back a candidate for mayor of Fortaleza, Brazil. That is what the campaign video showed. A court found that AI had been used to alter their likenesses and voices, giving a false impression of support. A correction can reach a voter after the ballot has been cast.

Europe

Who can afford Europe’s red tape?

You want to sell AI that screens CVs. EU rules call for documentation of how the system works and for human oversight. That is meant to protect job applicants. What if the cost of complying gives an edge to companies that already have entire legal departments?

Africa

The images came back after work

In Kenya, people helping to build an AI filter were expected to label up to 250 passages per shift. TIME reported take-home pay starting at about $1.32 an hour. One worker described how the image from a particularly disturbing passage kept coming back to him afterwards.

Asia

A relative gets a new number. You come under suspicion.

In Xinjiang, a system documented by Human Rights Watch flagged people as suspicious when a relative got a new phone number. That gave police a reason to investigate. How do you explain a decision somebody else made?

Australia and Oceania

Your voice without your consent

You record your grandmother’s voice to keep her words in the family. The same recording could train a model to speak in her voice. Te Hiku Media uses AI to transcribe te reo Māori and gives the guardians of recordings a say in how they are used again.

Antarctica

A catastrophe visible from orbit

Antarctica lost about 135 billion tonnes of ice a year on average from 2002 to 2025. That loss raises sea levels. AI helps us study the ice. When the electricity powering data centres comes from fossil fuels, the growth of that same technology adds emissions to the crisis it helps us observe.
